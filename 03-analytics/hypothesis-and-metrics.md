## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** In M2, my persona was the Frontline Driver. Mid-route, the app crashes, goes blank or shows a stale route, and the driver falls back to calls, WhatsApp or paper. "Mark delivered" takes 3 taps across 3 screens (BUG-2055, UXR-01). About 35% of proof-of-delivery uploads fail silently (BUG-2061). 5 of 7 drivers keep a paper manifest as a backup (UXR-12). All 7 drivers ranked core-action speed above new features.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** The M3 data only partly supports the driver findings. Drivers rate Core Dispatch 4.3/5 and use it daily (88%), so driver pain looks narrower than the interviews suggested. It doesn't disprove the crashes, but CSAT reflects active users and averages out occasional failures. The clearer signal is coordinators. They start compliance logging (77% adoption) but only 48% complete it in-app, it takes 14.6 min vs a 3-min benchmark, and they rate it 2.2/5. Coordinators lose 31 minutes a day to workarounds (up from about 9), and Coordinator NPS fell from +18 to −12. 69% abandon the workflow after route assignment. I hypothesize this is why drivers get stale reassignments and rely on WhatsApp. Based on this, I'm updating my persona to the Fleet Coordinator.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Fleet Coordinator, updated from the Frontline Driver after reconciling the M2 interviews with the M3 data. Goal: get every driver dispatched and close the shift on time, with compliance logged correctly. Confirmed friction: coordinators abandon the workflow partway, especially at compliance logging, where they lose the most time. Drivers feel the effect downstream when reassignments and handoffs move outside the app.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Compliance logging takes coordinators nearly 5× the benchmark time (14.6 vs 3 min), so half of them abandon it in RouteLogic and finish it outside the product, beyond its compliance controls.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Coordinators complete their daily workflow, including compliance logging, inside RouteLogic instead of in workarounds. Daily time lost falls, Coordinator NPS recovers, and drivers get timely, in-app updates instead of relying on WhatsApp. That reduces complexity-driven churn (now cited by 4 of 5 churned accounts) and protects the renewal of our largest account, which is trialing a leaner competitor.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The in-app compliance completion rate among coordinators. Target: from 48% to 70%.
Supporting signals:
- Median compliance step time down from 14.6 to 6 min or less.
- Daily time lost to workarounds down from 31 to under 20 min.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Compliance data completeness and accuracy must not drop below baseline. Manager Reporting CSAT must stay at or above 4.3 (currently 4.5). Managers are the buyers, and reporting is why customers buy RouteLogic, so simplifying coordinator compliance logging must not weaken the data their reports depend on.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** 6-week pilot: 2 weeks of ramp-up, then 4 weeks of measurement, with at least 30 coordinators.
Scale if in-app compliance completion reaches 65% or more and the guardrails hold. 
Pivot if completion rises but daily time lost doesn't fall, which would mean the bottleneck is elsewhere, such as shift handoff (31% completion, +6.8 min).
Kill if completion rises by less than 5 points or compliance data quality drops. 
If completion lands between 53% and 64%, iterate on the flow and extend the pilot by 4 weeks.
